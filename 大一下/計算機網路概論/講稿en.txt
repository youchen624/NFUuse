// Slide 2 - IEEE 802 LMSC
The committee was established in February nineteen eighteen.
Its name, IEEE 802, comes from the IEEE committee numbering system.
It is not based on the date of establishment, but the number does help people remember it.

The committee develops standards for Local Area Networks (LANs), Metropolitan Area Networks (MANs), and high-speed networks.
These two links provide more information about IEEE 802.

To handle this broad task, the committee is divided into several working groups.
Each group focuses on a specific area of networking technology.


// Slide 3 - Working Groups (tables)
These tables show the main IEEE 802 standards, which cover both wired and wireless local and metropolitan area networks.
The IEEE 802 family includes many networking standards, but here are the most important ones:

- **IEEE 802.3 (Ethernet)**: The most widely used wired LAN standard.
- **IEEE 802.11 (Wi-Fi)**: The foundation of nearly all wireless LANs.

Others like .1 (network management), .15 (Bluetooth), and .16 (WiMAX) also exist, but are either more technical or less common today.

// Slide 6 - IEEE 802 and OSI Model
IEEE 802 standards mostly operate at the first two layers of the OSI model:  
- **Layer 1**, the Physical Layer  
- **Layer 2**, the Data Link Layer

For example, IEEE 802.3 (Ethernet) and IEEE 802.11 (Wi-Fi) define how devices connect and how data is transferred over the network.  
Higher layers, like the Network and Transport layers, are handled by other protocols such as IP and TCP.

- **IEEE 802.15** supports wireless personal area networks, such as Bluetooth.

- **IEEE 802.1** manages the overall IEEE 802 architecture, including VLANs, bridging, and network security.  
- **IEEE 802.3**, or Ethernet, is the most widely used wired LAN standard in the world.  
- **IEEE 802.11**, also known as Wi-Fi, is the foundation of nearly all wireless LANs.
- **IEEE 802.18** advises on radio regulations to ensure standards meet global spectrum laws.

Together, these standards support the networking technologies we use every day and continue to evolve for future needs.
